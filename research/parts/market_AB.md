# Market research: Segments A and B

Access date for all sources: 2026-10-28, unless a different date is stated. Labels follow `research/RESEARCH_RULES.md`: FACT = seen on an official or primary page; VENDOR_CLAIM = a company's own marketing; CUSTOMER_OPINION = a review or forum post; HYPOTHESIS = our own interpretation. "snippet-only" means the claim was seen only in a search-result snippet and the page itself was not opened.

Scope: A = coaching institutes, training academies, and study-abroad or education consultancies. B = distributors, freight forwarders and customs brokers (CHAs), and SMBs with repetitive document processing. Focus is India, plus international.

**Main caveat up front:** Reddit was blocked for our search tool (HTTP 400, "domains not accessible"). G2, Capterra and TrustRadius returned 403. Customer-opinion evidence is therefore thinner than planned. It comes mostly from SoftwareFinder review pages, job postings, and survey or mystery-shopping data. Most "pain" statistics found are vendor claims.

## Segment A: Coaching institutes, academies and study-abroad consultancies

### A.1 Business model and customer profile (summary)
- **Indian coaching and test-prep institutes** sell courses and batches to students and parents. Leads come from Google and Meta ads, walk-ins, webinars, referrals and app downloads. Sales are done by tele-callers and counsellors who are paid a base salary plus incentives. Many institutes run several centres.
- **Study-abroad consultancies** earn fees from students and/or commissions from universities. Their work is long-cycle: enquiry, counselling, test prep, applications, documents, visa. Volume is seasonal around intakes.
- **International:** education agents worldwide show low digitisation of back-office work (ICEF survey below). Institutions' enquiry handling is weak on follow-up (Edified mystery-shopping data below).

### A.3 Common workflows and repetitive manual work
1. **Lead capture from many sources** (ads, website forms, JustDial-style portals, walk-ins, WhatsApp, events) into one list. This is often a shared Excel sheet (A13 VENDOR_CLAIM; A11 FACT for agents internationally).
2. **Lead assignment to a counsellor** by centre, course, language or country (A3, A13).
3. **First response and follow-up cadence:** calls, WhatsApp, reminders for demo classes or sessions (A15 job post; A12 shows follow-up is weak even at universities).
4. **Activity logging and daily reports** by counsellors (A15).
5. **Study-abroad specific:** document checklist collection, application status tracking per university, commission and invoice tracking (A11: 65% manual invoicing and Excel; 67% no commission software).
6. **Fee reminders and re-enrolment** (A14 VENDOR_CLAIM). Not researched further.

### A.4 Top workflows ranked (with reasoning)
1. **A-W1: Inquiry-to-enrolment follow-up automation.** Instant WhatsApp or email reply, scheduled nurture, reminder tasks for counsellors, and a daily summary, all sitting on top of the institute's existing sheet or CRM. *Reasoning:* the highest frequency (every lead, every day), with direct evidence that follow-up is weak (A12) and that job roles exist to do it manually (A15–A17). The weakness is that it is crowded (A1, A3, A7, A8, A21).
2. **A-W2: Study-abroad agency back-office.** Document checklist collection, application status tracking and commission/invoice tracking. *Reasoning:* the ICEF data shows large non-digitised gaps (A11), and incumbents focus on the front end (lead capture and nurture). *Weakness:* the survey is old (about 2022), workflows are less standardised, and buyers are fewer.
3. **A-W3: Lead assignment and counsellor accountability** (round-robin and skill-based routing, SLA alerts, missed-follow-up escalation). Very common, but it is now a checkbox feature: Interakt Sales CRM at ₹2,499/mo (A7), Zoho assignment rules (A9), Wati Business (A8). Best sold as part of A-W1.
4. **A-W4: Fee reminder and re-enrolment campaigns.** Likely common, but no direct evidence was gathered (HYPOTHESIS).

### A.7 Conflicting evidence (Segment A)
- LeadSquared pricing: the official page shows ₹90k–1.49L/yr per account for 2 users. LeadSquared's own blog shows per-user USD pricing and claims a "one-time payment" (A1 vs A2).

- Reviews on Meritto are mixed: several reviewers report "no cons" (A5).

## Segment B: Distributors, freight forwarders and CHAs, and document-heavy SMBs

### B.1 Business model and customer profile (summary)
- **Indian distributors and traders:** buy from manufacturers and sell to retailers. They run on Tally (very common) or Busy or Zoho Books. Purchase bills arrive by email or paper. Orders arrive via salesmen and WhatsApp. GST compliance (e-invoicing at AATO above ₹5 Cr, GSTR-2B matching) adds data-accuracy pressure.
- **Freight forwarders and CHAs:** earn per shipment. Each shipment needs BL/HBL, invoice, packing list, shipping bill or bill of entry, and certificate of origin. CHAs file on ICEGATE. Larger players use CargoWise; Indian SMBs use local software such as Logi-Sys.
- **International SMBs:** similar AP and invoice data entry into QuickBooks or Xero, and BOL entry into a TMS.

### B.3 Common workflows and repetitive manual work
1. **Purchase bill / vendor invoice to Tally (or Zoho Books, Busy):** download from email or WhatsApp, key in the fields, map ledgers and GST, post (B5, B6).
2. **GSTR-2B vs purchase register reconciliation:** monthly. Not deeply researched here; TaxOne lists GST API import (B3).
3. **Sales order capture from WhatsApp, salesmen or phone into Tally** (distributors) (B28, B29).
4. **Freight and CHA: shipment document data entry.** BL/HBL, invoice and packing list data are keyed into forwarding software, into the CHA checklist for BE/SB, and into billing (B20, B24–B27).
5. **AP matching of carrier/vendor invoices to shipments** (B23, B24 VENDOR_CLAIM).

### B.4 Top workflows ranked (with reasoning)
1. **B-W1: Purchase invoice / bill extraction into Tally with ledger and GST mapping and an exception queue.** *Reasoning:* this is the most repeatable and most frequent workflow, the Tally base is huge, and the work is concrete and demoable. *But:* very cheap incumbents exist (TaxOne ₹10k/yr unlimited, B3; Zoho Books autoscans, B4), Tally has a native Excel import (B2), and a CA has published a DIY tool (B5). The defensible part is the "last mile" (B6), not OCR.
2. **B-W2: BL and shipping-document data entry for SMB freight forwarders and CHAs** (extract BL/invoice/packing list into a job sheet, CHA checklist or Excel for upload to existing software). *Reasoning:* there is higher value per document. Documentation staff are paid more (B25 snippet ₹40–60k/mo) and errors cause costly delays (B20 VENDOR_CLAIM). Enterprise tools (Rossum $18k/yr minimum, B18; Raft and Expedock by demo only) are priced above Indian SMBs. *But:* local forwarding software is adding AI (Logi-Sys LogiLENS, B24), integration targets vary, and buyer access is harder.
3. **B-W3: Distributor WhatsApp order to Tally sales order.** It is frequent, but DMS products already do it for ₹208/user/mo (B28) and Tally has native order management (B29).
4. **B-W4: GSTR-2B reconciliation.** Not scored. There was insufficient research, and many GST tools likely compete (HYPOTHESIS).

### B.6 How they solve it today
- Manual entry by an accounts executive, data entry operator or documentation executive (B8, B9, B25–B27).
- Tally's native Excel import plus Excel cleanup (B2, B6).
- Low-cost Tally add-ons: TaxOne/Suvit (B3), with switching to Zoho Books autoscan as an alternative (B4).
- Generic IDP SaaS (Nanonets, Docsumo, Parseur, Docparser, KlearStack, Rossum) (B13–B18).
- Freight-specific: AI built into the forwarding software (Logi-Sys LogiLENS) or enterprise AI vendors (Raft) (B23, B24).
- DIY scripts by CAs (B5) and agencies (B30).

### B.7 Conflicting evidence (Segment B)
- "Manual BOL entry persists" (B20) vs "93% of documents fully automated" (B23). Both are vendor claims and serve different customer sizes.
- Expedock was a cited CargoWise automation vendor (B21, 2022), but its current homepage sells managed teams (B22). The AI-only model may not have been enough on its own. HYPOTHESIS.
- TaxOne's "unlimited" plan for ₹10k/yr (B3) vs Zoho's per-scan pricing (B4) vs Nanonets' $0.30/extraction (B13). The market price per document varies by roughly 100x.
- E-invoicing thresholds: the official portal page did not state the ₹5 Cr threshold. It was confirmed only via a secondary legal summary (B10, B12).

## Cross-segment notes
- **General automation tools** (n8n self-hosted is free, A19; Zapier from $19.99/mo, A20) make it cheap for agencies and freelancers to build both workflows. Barriers to entry are low.
- **Agency price anchors in India** (A21, A22, B30): ₹35k–7L builds plus ₹8k/mo. A ₹10–30k pilot sits well below the published agency floor. That is a positive for winning pilots, but a warning about perceived value. HYPOTHESIS.
- **Security and regulation:** DPDP core obligations apply from about May 2027 (A23). GST e-invoicing and 30-day IRP limits (B10, B11) make invoice data accuracy compliance-relevant. ICEGATE filing stays with the CHA (B32, HYPOTHESIS).
